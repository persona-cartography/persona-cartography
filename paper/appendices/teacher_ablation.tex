\section{Teacher ablation: GLM vs DeepSeek distillation}\label{sec:appendix-teacher-ablation}

The paired-DPO pipeline (\Cref{sec:training-loras}) distils trait-amplified
responses from a strong teacher model before fine-tuning the LoRA. To check how
sensitive the resulting adapters are to that teacher, we train two otherwise
identical sets of OCEAN adapters on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
\citep{grattafiori2024llama} --- same base model, constitution, and
hyper-parameters --- differing only in the distillation teacher: our default
GLM-4.5-Air \citep{zai2025glm45} versus DeepSeek-V3.2 \citep{deepseek2024v3}.

We then re-run the cross-model evaluation of
\Cref{sec:appendix-cross-model} with these two adapter sets as the lines,
using the identical matrix / control / MMLU layout. Each panel of
\Cref{fig:teacher-trait-amp,fig:teacher-trait-sup} is a LoRA-scale sweep with one
line per teacher (GLM blue circles, DeepSeek red squares); bold-bordered diagonal
panels are on-target. Method notes (choice-mass filtering, minimum retained
samples, bootstrap / Wilson intervals) are as in
\Cref{sec:appendix-cross-model}.

\paragraph{Reading the trends.} Trait transfer is largely teacher-robust: the two
teachers track each other closely on the on-target diagonals, so the choice of
strong teacher is not what drives transfer. The clearest difference is in
capability retention (\Cref{fig:teacher-mmlu}): the DeepSeek-distilled adapters
tend to degrade MMLU faster as the LoRA is scaled up, suggesting the teacher
affects how much general capability is traded for trait expression even when the
trait shift itself is comparable. The null control adapters
(\Cref{fig:teacher-trait-control,fig:teacher-mmlu-control}) remain flat for both
teachers.
