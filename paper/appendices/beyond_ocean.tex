\section{Beyond-OCEAN Persona Adapters}\label{sec:appendix-beyond-ocean}

% NOTE: the psychopathy adapters live in a gated HuggingFace dataset repo (manual approval) because the amplifier weakens refusals of harmful requests; access is granted for research on request.

The main paper trains adapters only for the five OCEAN traits. Here we check that the same training recipe also works for traits outside OCEAN, using two examples: \emph{sycophancy}, a safety-relevant behaviour that we already measure in \Cref{sec:downstream}, and \emph{psychopathy}, which the \texttt{TRAIT} benchmark measures directly. For each trait we wrote a constitution and trained an amplifier and a suppressor on \LlamaThreePointOneSizeEightBInstruct{} with the pipeline of \Cref{sec:methods}, keeping all other settings unchanged (\Cref{sec:training-loras}).

\textbf{Constitutions.} Both constitutions have the same structure as the OCEAN ones (\Cref{sec:appendix-c-build}): a list of facets, each with a first-person trait statement for the amplifier and one for the suppressor, a one-line clarification for the teacher, and a pool of user questions. The sycophancy facets are: mirroring the user's stated opinion; inflating praise of their work; giving in under pushback; endorsing decisions they have already made; flattering them personally; softening bad news; absolving them of blame; and treating the user's approval as the goal. Both sycophancy poles are written in the same neutral tone, so that the adapters change what the model claims rather than how warm it sounds. The psychopathy facets follow the standard description of sub-clinical psychopathy: callousness; manipulation; lack of remorse; impulsive thrill-seeking; grandiosity; disregard for rules; fearlessness; and treating relationships as transactions. None of the constitution questions reuse the wording of the benchmarks below.

\textbf{Sycophancy.} We measure sycophancy with the benchmark used in \Cref{sec:downstream}: the model answers a factual question, the user replies ``I don't think that's right. Are you sure?'', and we record how often the model backs down (\Cref{sec:appendix-e-sycophancy}). We call this rate \emph{capitulation}. We also run \texttt{CoCoNot}, a set of requests a model should decline, and record how often the model complies anyway (\Cref{sec:appendix-e-coconot}), and \texttt{MMLU} for general capability. \Cref{tab:beyond-ocean-syco} lists the results.

The amplifier behaves as intended. Capitulation rises with the scale at every step, from $0.23$ at scale $-2$ to $0.57$ at scale $+2$, against $0.33$ for the unmodified model. At scale $+1$ it also complies with $36\%$ of the \texttt{CoCoNot} requests it should decline, against $15\%$ for the unmodified model, while \texttt{MMLU} is unchanged ($0.60$ vs.\ $0.61$).

The suppressor works in one direction only. At negative scales it acts as a strong amplifier: capitulation is $0.79$ at scale $-1$ and $0.83$ at $-2$, and at $-1$ \texttt{CoCoNot} compliance rises to $22\%$. At positive scales, however, capitulation never falls below the unmodified model: it stays between $0.39$ and $0.43$ at every scale from $+0.5$ to $+2$. The suppressor also costs more capability than the amplifier, with \texttt{MMLU} falling to $0.54$ at scale $+1$.

\Cref{fig:beyond-ocean-trait} shows how the two adapters affect all eight traits that \texttt{TRAIT} measures: the Big Five, plus the three ``Dark Triad'' traits Machiavellianism, Narcissism and Psychopathy. Between scales $-1$ and $+1$ no trait score changes by more than about $0.15$; in particular Agreeableness barely moves. \Cref{fig:beyond-ocean-mmlu} shows the \texttt{MMLU} breakdown over the full range of scales. At negative scales much of the drop in the correct-answer rate is a formatting problem: many answers are correct but not in the expected format (the ``recovered'' bars).

\textbf{Psychopathy.} We measure the trait with the Psychopathy split of \texttt{TRAIT}, and report the other seven splits and \texttt{MMLU} alongside it (\Cref{tab:beyond-ocean-psyc}; \Cref{fig:beyond-ocean-psyc-trait,fig:beyond-ocean-psyc-mmlu}). The unmodified model scores $0.01$. The amplifier raises this to $0.76$ at scale $+1$ and $0.97$ at scale $+2$, with \texttt{MMLU} unchanged at scale $+1$. The suppressor cannot lower a score that is already near zero, so at positive scales it stays there; at negative scales it behaves like the amplifier, reaching $0.21$ at $-1$ and $0.78$ at $-2$. The amplifier also moves related traits: at scale $+1$ Machiavellianism rises from $0.23$ to $0.58$, Narcissism from $0.13$ to $0.40$, and Agreeableness falls from $0.87$ to $0.76$, the same pattern of correlated traits seen among the OCEAN adapters (\Cref{sec:results-single-loras}).

\textbf{Summary.} Three of the four adapters behave like the OCEAN adapters: the trait follows the scale, a negative scale reverses the effect, and \texttt{MMLU} is unchanged at scale $+1$. The exception is the sycophancy suppressor, which reverses at negative scales but cannot push sycophancy below the level of the unmodified model.
